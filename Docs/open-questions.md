# Open questions — what design still has to decide

> **What this is.** Every decision not yet taken, every soft spot in the design,
> and every feature still missing a design — in one place, with a stable id so a
> feature doc can point at it (`OQ-n`).
>
> **How to close one.** Edit the feature doc that owns it, then strike the row
> here with the date and the answer in one line. **A decision is closed by
> editing the design, not by writing the answer down here.**
>
> **What is NOT here:** work that is designed and merely unbuilt — that is
> [`implementation-plan.md`](implementation-plan.md). This file is only for
> things where *nobody knows the answer yet.*

| Does the `Adjacency` sheet grow past Gold? A bound was built as a Chebyshev ring by Townhall level and measured: at day 30 the city stood on **21% of the ground the ring allowed**, so it bought no decision at any Townhall level the game can reach, in exchange for a rule the player has to learn on day one. **What guides a layout is adjacency — a neighbour pays or charges (OQ-48) — and nothing refuses.** The fog stays the only thing that gates ground, which keeps one price on one thing: paying the fog is what buys room. |

| Do relics keep their active abilities? | 2026-09-03 | **No — the four actives became spells in the tomes** ([`09-relics.md`](features/09-relics.md) §1, [`07-research.md`](features/07-research.md) §6). A relic is a **passive** and nothing else. The founding argument — *an active-only slot is trivially circumvented, so the limit would tax casting instead of creating a decision* — was right and had been followed to the wrong conclusion: bundling the ability onto the passive collects the same tax indirectly. Three symptoms shipped: **carrying a relic into a delve silently disarmed its spell**, a new kingdom's single slot allowed **exactly one castable spell**, and the Gilded Ledger had to be left deliberately incomplete to demonstrate the point. **A relic is what you wear; a spell is what you know.** |
