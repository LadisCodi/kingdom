# Closed questions — the decisions on record

> **What this is.** Decisions already taken, kept only while the reasoning is
> still worth having to hand. The design itself lives in the feature docs; this
> is the ledger, never the authority.
>
> **What is NOT here:** anything still undecided — that is
> [`open-questions.md`](open-questions.md).

Kept short, and only while the reasoning is still worth having to hand.

| Question | Closed | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Is the world visibly harsher than home, or the same palette? (**OQ-66**) | 2026-09-24 | **One palette, one terrain set, one light** ([`art/art-direction.md`](art/art-direction.md) §2). It was framed as a budget decision and the budget decided it: a warm home against a severe world means **two terrain sets to make and maintain**, and the contrast it buys is bought more cheaply by fog and zoom. What separates the two scales is the **camera**, not the colour — the city is an isometric diorama you look into, the board is a flat map you read — and what carries continuity across them is the palette, the light and the silhouettes. |
| Is the world a sparse procedural territory or a small authored board? | 2026-09-24 | **A board: 91 hexes, radius 5, six players** ([`19-world-map.md`](features/19-world-map.md) §1). The two live designs had drifted into two different games — `02` specified a large procedural lattice where *the empty hexes are the cost of travel* and 15–20 place compositions of art, while `19` specified rings with assigned roles around a Dark Portal where every hex has a job. The board wins on three counts: **conflict becomes a property of the geometry** rather than a rule, the art budget is a fraction, and it is the half that was already designed to depth. The large lattice is not deferred, it is dropped. `02` gives up designing the world and shrinks to what it is — the scopes and the authority boundary. |
| How is the world explored? | 2026-09-24 | **You send an army, and it marches** ([`19-world-map.md`](features/19-world-map.md) §3.1). Not the Gold-plus-timer `19` had specified, which was the province's tap wearing a costume and made the second scope read as the first with a worse camera. An army reveals its hex and the six around it at every hex it crosses, so a march opens a corridor. **It costs no Gold** — Gold's sink out here is building improvements, and charging the march too would charge twice for one decision. |
| What is the balancing lever for the whole board? | 2026-09-24 | **Armies per player, and how long casualties take to replace** ([`19-world-map.md`](features/19-world-map.md) §4). Exploring, claiming, attacking, holding a corridor and diving the Portal all draw on one bag of troops, so choosing any of them is choosing against the other four. When the board feels too quiet or too violent that is the number to move — not the inner ring's +200%, not the march times. Nothing else throttles conflict: there is no rule forbidding it, only the price in troops. |
| Where are the specific spellbooks found? | 2026-09-24 | **Basic books in the province's ruins, rare books in the outer ring's dungeons** ([`19-world-map.md`](features/19-world-map.md) §8). A player who never contests the board still has a complete route through research; the world **widens** what a kingdom can become without monopolising it. This makes live the option `02` §5 had floated and left undesigned — technologies as an exclusive output of world exploration. |
| Does a claimed world hex open as a temporary province? (**OQ-5**) | 2026-09-24 | **No.** A dungeon is a destination you send expeditions into, never a third map level, and a hexagon never opens a map of its own ([`19-world-map.md`](features/19-world-map.md) §13). Merging conquest and events into one system was elegant and is more than the prototype needs. |
| Is world travel cost linear in hexes or superlinear? (**OQ-65**) | 2026-09-24 | **Linear — Y hexes cost X·Y** ([`19-world-map.md`](features/19-world-map.md) §4). Superlinear scaling exists to stop a large map collapsing once speed upgrades land, and the board is ten hexes across at its widest. Legible head arithmetic wins where there is no large map to protect. |
| Flat-top or pointy-top hexagons? (**OQ-67**) | 2026-09-24 | **Pointy-top**, as the renders came out and as they read in portrait ([`19-world-map.md`](features/19-world-map.md) §1). The axial maths is written once; this settles it before it is. |
| Are the three tomes the whole of research? | 2026-09-24 | **No — three general books are the spine, and specific books are found.** The three that exist (Civics, Warfare, Magic) stay open to every kingdom from the start. On top of them sit **narrow books the player finds** — at the bottom of a ruin, out of an event, on the world map — each doing one thing no general book does. Personalisation comes from **which books you own and in what order**, never from a renunciation: nothing is locked away by choosing. This supersedes the 2026-09-03 row below, which settled the count at three and nothing else. It makes a ruin able to pay **the only reward that changes how the game is played rather than how fast**, and it turns `TomeId` from a code union into authored content ([`07-research.md`](features/07-research.md), [`tech-tree-editor.md`](tech-tree-editor.md)). |
| Does the world map ever allow another player to raid a city? (**OQ-2**) | 2026-09-24 | **A claimed hex, yes. A city, never.** Promise 1 is reworded rather than dropped: *your city can never be attacked; everything outside it can be.* What a player can take from another player is territory on the shared world map — hexes that were claimed and can be lost — and nothing that stands in the province. The province keeps its own bounded, recoverable garrison raids ([`18-garrisons-and-raids.md`](features/18-garrisons-and-raids.md)) and gains nothing new. This is the fork that makes the game a 4X rather than a cozy idle builder, and it is said out loud in the pitch. |
| What is a relic, and how is it levelled? | 2026-09-09 | **A permanent kingdom passive with no ceiling, levelled by completing card albums in a shared season** ([`09-relics.md`](features/09-relics.md)). Attunement is cut — every relic the player has is on — because with five economic passives "wear them all" always won and a slot only delayed it, and because a player who pays to level a relic they cannot wear is a refund waiting to happen. Stardust and the Fragments gate leave the relics with it. The season is what makes the collection social: a card is wiped at the close, so a duplicate is free to give, which is the mechanism that makes Monopoly Go's trading work and the one the first two drafts of this design were missing. An album pays three things — the level, a chest priced in hours of production, keys and Gems — because a level alone is a slope and a slope does not sell a pack; the collection prize is the season hero and 25,000 Gems, dealt in the gacha reveal. The Rod and the Seal stop being discounts, which cannot scale, and take one harvest clock each. |
| Does a cleared gate return its hoard in full? (**OQ-74**) | 2026-09-09 | **In full, banked the moment the garrison falls.** It is the sentence the reopened promise 1 rests on — *nothing is taken that you cannot take back* — and a fraction would have bought teeth that outlive the fight at the price of the sentence. Built with the rest of the gate. |
| Is promise 1 — *nothing you own is ever taken* — reopened, and for what? | 2026-09-08 | **Yes, deliberately, for exactly one thing: a garrison the player has seen and left standing** ([`18-garrisons-and-raids.md`](features/18-garrisons-and-raids.md)). The promise now reads *no other player can ever touch your city*. What made the reopening acceptable is the three bounds written into the design: a raid is priced in the city's own production and capped at a tenth of the purse, a garrison makes at most three trips, and **everything it took comes back when it is cleared**. What it buys is the genre's clearest objective — *defend your village* — as the doorway to combat, on the surface, with the enemy in view and the first outcome guaranteed. **OQ-2 stays no**: a *player* raid is the other fork, and it is still shut. |
| How is a defended landmark cleared? (**OQ-35**) | 2026-09-08 | **By a hero and a party, solo, through the expedition sheet** — and every site has a garrison, not four. The weekly co-op siege keeps its design as the world map's first place. |
| Is the relic system behind a building? | 2026-09-08 | **No — the Reliquary is cut.** A relic is FOUND, at the bottom of a ruin, and the mechanic unlocks on owning one: the Relics nav tab is hidden until the player has a relic and appears the moment they do ([`09-relics.md`](features/09-relics.md) §1.1). The builder programme had proposed a 1×1 district whose ten levels capped a relic's level, and asking a player to go and build something before they may look at the first relic they were handed is a gate in the wrong place. **The cut costs three things**, and two of them were already only designed: the relic level-cap ladder, ingredient trading, and — the one that matters — the weekly Runestone recipe for a 3★ ingredient, which was the written answer to **OQ-7**. That question is open again and wider. |
| What is a garrison, once combat is the resolver? | 2026-09-08 | **A gate: one garrison room before a ruin's Depth 1, with a raid counter** ([`18-garrisons-and-raids.md`](features/18-garrisons-and-raids.md)). Discovering the ruin starts the clock; the player attacks it as a room; if the clock runs out the garrison takes a bounded, recoverable slice of banked materials **without a fight** — nothing at home defends, and the roster exists to attack. Landmarks lose their garrisons and are claimed for Gold; the first fight is the Hollow Barrow's gate. The home defence, the raid sheet, the march and the rousing conditions are all cut: it is a small personal event with a clock whose job is to send the player into the ruin. |
| What is a hero on the resolver's board, and what levels it? | 2026-09-08 | **A body and a type buff, nothing else — for the prototype.** A hero fights from a hero slot at ~70% of a squad and its one passive buffs the troops of its own type (`dmg`, `hp`, flat `def`); no ultimate, no energy, no economy traits, no party-wide stat ([`10-heroes.md`](features/10-heroes.md) §2, [`combat.md`](features/combat.md) §9). **Hero XP is a kingdom currency** spent on any hero's levels; **ascension costs that hero's Fragments plus a Stardust toll**, and Stardust keeps the relics. **One hero slot is free and every further one is Gems, always**; the Guild opens depths, never slots (OQ-83 closed). The hero-only battle mode is cut — every fight fields troops and heroes; a hero arena is a possible future. The Legendary *Scout* is renamed *Ranger* so the Guild's threat preview owns the word. |
| Do heroes need a building, and where does the banner live? | 2026-09-08 | **Yes, the Tavern — and the banner moves into it.** Heroes are unlocked by the building and **called by tapping it**, the way tapping the Market opens the trade screen ([`10-heroes.md`](features/10-heroes.md) §8). The store loses its Heroes section and keeps what Gems are BOUGHT with; a pull is still Gems, spent at the Tavern. The asymmetry with the relics above is deliberate: a relic is something the player already holds when the screen opens, while a hero is something they go and ask for, so a destination to walk to is what a hero wants and what a relic does not. |
| What does Harmony cost, now the plot is unbounded? | 2026-09-08 | **A count cap per decoration, and a good in every piece past the Garden** ([`../plans/builder-30-days.md`](plans/builder-30-days.md) §6). Harmony stays a **city total** — supply − demand, computed on read, a gate and never a drain — because with **OQ-1** closed the other way a decoration no longer competes for ground, and per-building reach would have made a placement REFUSABLE, which **OQ-48** says it may never be. What replaces the ground as the scarce thing is **variety and the workshop queue**: each of the six pieces has its own `max_count_per_townhall_level`, so reaching a Townhall's demand needs several KINDS, and every kind past the Garden is priced in a refined good. Where a piece stands is paid for by adjacency (`Housing ↔ AnyDecoration +1 Gold/min`), not by the gate. |
| Where does the Harmony surplus bonus land? | 2026-09-08 | **On housing taxes, as proposed** — three tiers of `supply / demand`, +5 / +10 / +15% at 1.10 / 1.25 / 1.50, a base-stage term in `effectiveTaxRate`. The 30-day harness ends on **16.8 M unspent Gold**, so this is a legibility win rather than a real reward, and it is authored to be cheap to move: the tiers are one setting and the stat is one call site. **At demand 0 there is no ratio and no bonus** — otherwise one Garden at Townhall 5 pays the top tier for the whole midgame, free. |
| Does the `Adjacency` sheet grow past Gold? (**OQ-48**) | 2026-09-07 | **Yes: `(district, neighbour, stat, magnitude)`, and `gold_per_minute` is gone rather than kept beside it** — one mechanism, not two ([`03-economy.md`](features/03-economy.md) §3.1). **Either side may name a KIND** (`AnyHall`, `AnyWorkshop`, `AnyProducer`), derived from what a district already is, so "a hall beside another hall" is one row instead of twelve and a fifth hall would need none. Every fractional stat is clamped to **±25%**, which is what keeps a layout better-or-worse rather than right-or-wrong — and it has to, because with OQ-1 closed the other way adjacency is the ONLY thing guiding a layout and it may never be worth refusing a spot over. **The rule the step established:** a rate is computed on read, a **timer is priced when it starts and stamped on the thing waiting**, so a neighbour that moves never repriced a wait already running. |
| Is the province plot bounded? (**OQ-1**) | 2026-09-07 | **No. A building goes anywhere the player has revealed**, and the Housing rule that required a Townhall or another house edge-to-edge is gone with it ([`05-city-and-districts.md`](features/05-city-and-districts.md) §4). A bound was built as a Chebyshev ring by Townhall level and measured: at day 30 the city stood on **21% of the ground the ring allowed**, so it bought no decision at any Townhall level the game can reach, in exchange for a rule the player has to learn on day one. **What guides a layout is adjacency — a neighbour pays or charges (OQ-48) — and nothing refuses.** The fog stays the only thing that gates ground, which keeps one price on one thing: paying the fog is what buys room. |
| Where do plot expansions come from? (**OQ-71**) | 2026-09-07 | **Moot — there is nothing to expand.** The plot is the revealed province (OQ-1), so room is bought by paying the fog, and the world map's outputs lose a row. |
| What does the daily chest pay, and what does a streak add? | 2026-09-02 | **Mana every day, Gems at the week marker only.** Gems daily devalues the marker and makes the faucet hard to bound. And **the ladder advances on days *played***, so a streak cannot be lost — which is what keeps it inside promise 1. |
| Does the daily pill auto-open? | 2026-09-02 | **No, it only glows.** The design is about a game that does not make demands; a build that opens with one contradicts it in the first second. |
| How many tomes, and what are they? | 2026-09-03 | **Three: Civics, Warfare, Magic** ([`07-research.md`](features/07-research.md) §2) — down from a proposed six, because three is what a tab strip holds on a phone and what makes each tome a place rather than a thin page. **Magic's remit is the decision that makes it work:** not *spells*, but **any effect the fiction can carry as enchantment**, so it holds economic nodes too — *the trees grow back because you sang to them.* That gives the shelf what §2 says the tree lacks: **Civics is the mundane route to an outcome and Magic is the enchanted route to the same one**, a real choice with real opportunity cost and no lock. Bounded by one rule: **two tomes may aim at the same outcome, never move the same stat.** |
| What shape is a tome's page? | 2026-09-03 | **A vertical spine, at most three parallel columns, with branch and join nodes — and a tier gate is a join node** ([`07-research.md`](features/07-research.md) §2.1, §2.2). Tiers are **bands on one scrolling page**, not separate pages, so the next era is visible before it is reachable. **The payoff was going to be that the layout stops being authored** — a derived row, no `node: {x, y}` — and **that half was answered the other way on 2026-09-07**: the layout stays authored and got an editor (`?dev=tree`, [`tech-tree-editor.md`](tech-tree-editor.md)), because a designer who cannot say *this branch reads left of that one* is accepting a tree rather than laying one out. What made hand-authoring risky — collisions, a connector through a node — is now checked as you drag, on save, and in CI. |
| Do relics keep their active abilities? | 2026-09-03 | **No — the four actives became spells in the tomes** ([`09-relics.md`](features/09-relics.md) §1, [`07-research.md`](features/07-research.md) §6). A relic is a **passive** and nothing else. The founding argument — *an active-only slot is trivially circumvented, so the limit would tax casting instead of creating a decision* — was right and had been followed to the wrong conclusion: bundling the ability onto the passive collects the same tax indirectly. Three symptoms shipped: **carrying a relic into a delve silently disarmed its spell**, a new kingdom's single slot allowed **exactly one castable spell**, and the Gilded Ledger had to be left deliberately incomplete to demonstrate the point. **A relic is what you wear; a spell is what you know.** |
| What is a tome, and what generates Knowledge? | 2026-09-03 | **A tome is a page of technologies** — you pour into a node, never into a tome — and **the Knowledge drip is flat and ungated**, with cleared ruins raising the **cap** rather than the rate ([`07-research.md`](features/07-research.md) §3, §2). Today's generator returns zero with no cleared ruin, which would have put **the entire technology tree behind a dungeon** and turned OQ-41's risk into the shape of the game. |
| Which currency is called Knowledge? | 2026-09-03 | **The research clock, and the collection becomes Stardust** — which was already the written design in twelve docs while the code had zero occurrences of the word ([`07-research.md`](features/07-research.md) §4). The scopes swap with the names: Stardust is kingdom-scoped so it survives a region reset, the new Knowledge is city-scoped like Mana. **The migrator is the easy direction** — the balance keeps its meaning and changes its name, so no rate conversion — **and the danger is only in doing nothing**, which would silently turn a live player's collection savings into free research. |
| Does a Wonder stand on the province, competing for ground? | 2026-09-03 | **Yes, with a footprint deliberately larger than it needs to be** ([`16-wonders.md`](features/16-wonders.md) §5.1). A precinct outside the plot was the cheaper answer and it makes the footprint decoration. **The ground is the half of a Wonder's price that is not Gold**, and it is the half that keeps costing after the level is bought — and with **OQ-1** closed the other way (2026-09-07) what a big footprint costs is the fog that revealed it and the adjacency it displaces. |
| What absorbs the surplus once every ladder is bought? | 2026-09-03 | **Buildings with no last level, fed with Gold on an exponential curve** — Elvenar's Ancient Wonders ([`16-wonders.md`](features/16-wonders.md)). The measurement that settled it: the game holds ~**29,100,000 Gold** of sink (28,517,245 fog, 527,000 landmarks, 51,926 upgrades, 6,600 tree) and **every coin of it is one-time**, so the end of the province is the end of the economy. The fault was the shape, not the size. |
| Do orders live on the Market, and do they exist before it? | 2026-09-03 | **Moot — orders were cut from the design entirely** ([`12-quests.md`](features/12-quests.md) §6). The case for them priced the problem against the smallest Gold sink in the game. What survives is the rule they were holding: a sink must never pay back what it asked for. |
| Is the Gem faucet over budget? | 2026-09-02 | **No.** 10 start + 15 chain + 50 first-clears = **75**, exactly the budget. The claim of 110 assumed eleven quests carried Gem rewards; four do. Now asserted by a test. |
| Should relics cost Mana upkeep? | 2026-09-02 | **No — upkeep removed entirely.** At Townhall 1 the full set drew exactly what the Townhall made, so wearing everything stalled the pool dead. **Attune-or-arm rests on exclusivity alone, and a cost you cannot out-produce is a firmer constraint than one you can.** |
| Do landmarks raise Mana production or capacity? | 2026-09-02 | **Capacity, +10 each.** A rate bonus is worth most on the day you find it and less every day after; capacity is worth *more* the longer you play, and it doubles every future ad. **The same argument now governs contested Knowledge landmarks.** |
| Should the Mana pool refill exactly overnight? | 2026-09-02 | **No — the tuning law is suspended by decision.** It belonged to an *absence* budget; Mana is a *spend* budget now, and **a spend budget has to be able to run out or a refill has nothing to sell.** |
| Is there a waiting line for builds? | 2026-09-02 | **No.** A waiting line is administratively convenient and dramatically inert — the player queues five things and never meets the constraint. **Without one the constraint has a moment, and a moment is something a game can build on.** |
| Is an iron vein just a mountain with different art? | 2026-09-03 | **Yes, and it is gone.** `IronVein` and the `Iron` source were deleted; the metal is a *kind of mountain* now — **iron** pays Stone at five times a bare peak, **gold** pays Gold at three. Both are worked by the Mine, which had been a second Quarry pointed at a second rock ever since iron stopped being a currency. The redundancy went away by making the two things genuinely different rather than by deleting one of them. |
| Should the map be authored in the workbook? | 2026-09-03 | **No.** A map is not tabular data. Terrain, features, landmarks and ruins live in a JSON document painted in `?dev=map`; the workbook keeps every *number*. |
| Is the world map a node graph or a lattice? | 2026-09-04 | **A hex lattice, and the hex is a unit of measure, not a container** ([`19-world-map.md`](features/19-world-map.md) §1.1). The hexagon exists so that distance is countable and reveal is bounded; places are sparse tenants of it, and the empty hexes between them *are* the cost of travel. Two rounds of mockups settled it — an earlier draft had argued the geometry from icon legibility, which was backwards. |
| How big is a world-map hex? | 2026-09-04 | **Two registers, ~3× apart** ([`19-world-map.md`](features/19-world-map.md) §1.2): a **tactical** ~130 pt / ~3 across to look at a place, a **strategic** ~45 pt / ~8–9 across to count hexes and plan. The strategic view is the planning surface, not an overview, so it ships with the world map. Three or four content elements read comfortably at the tactical size. |
| Are the icons on a world hex tappable? | 2026-09-04 | **No — icons are read, the hexagon is tapped, and actions live in a dispatch sheet.** At ~130 pt an icon lands at 25–40 pt, under the 44 pt / 48 dp minimums. **A hexagon never opens a map of its own** — a nested interior would be a third map level. |
| How many fog states does the world map have? | 2026-09-04 | **Three, matching the province's Undiscovered / Discovered / Revealed** ([`19-world-map.md`](features/19-world-map.md) §3): unknown mist, a half-veiled *sensed* band with silhouettes showing through, and revealed ground. The middle band is what makes the fog a place to go rather than a grey void. |
| How much does a world scout reveal? | 2026-09-04 | **One hex, then it grows** ([`19-world-map.md`](features/19-world-map.md) §3.1): radius 0 → 1 (seven hexes) → 2 (nineteen), as a progression axis. Starting at one keeps continuity with the province's one-tap fog and makes each reveal a decision; the ring opens before one-at-a-time starts to grate. **And world fog is per-player and client-side** — it gates nothing, so there is nothing to cheat (§4.2). |
| Is €20/month the right simulated budget? | 2026-09-04 | **Not one budget — five, set by a payer profile the playtester picks once** ([`14-monetization.md`](features/14-monetization.md) §3): F2P $0, Minnow $10, Dolphin $50, Whale $250, Super Whale $2,000 a calendar month, never rolling over. The five are the genre's own fish scale, so a ranking by profile reads against the comparables' revenue mix. The profile is final for the save; starting over asks again. |
| Do the Gem sinks scale to the Gem ladder? (OQ-27) | 2026-09-04 | **Yes — every sink repriced to 500 Gems a dollar, on Kingshot's anchors** ([`14-monetization.md`](features/14-monetization.md) §9): a pull 1,000, the second builder 2,500 (`×2`), a full Mana refill 500, an hour of rush 720, slots 2,500 / 1,500 / 1,000 with `×2` growth. The faucet scaled ×50 with them (3,750 up front, ~1,000 a month), so the first rung of every ladder is still earned by play. The refill-against-pull pairing that the question started as is settled by the same move: a refill is half a pull. |
| Is 30 Gems right for the second builder? (OQ-30) | 2026-09-04 | **Moot — it is 2,500 now, the $4.99 pack to the coin,** which is what Kingshot charges for the same thing. Whether the refused-build offer arrives too early is still a playtest question, but it is no longer a pricing one. |
